# Harar visual research brief

Compiled 2026-11-25 by a research subagent from academic, UNESCO-derived, museum and press sources, then
reviewed by the producer. It is used to write prompts and to review keyframes. **This is general context, not
family history.** Family-specific details are listed at the bottom as open questions.

UNESCO's own pages returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches, so UNESCO facts come via OVPM's copy of the
UNESCO description.

## Do not depict / avoid (used in `meta.negative_prompt` and in reviews)
- Hyena feeding (a tourist cliché).
- Moroccan or Arabian medina tropes: horseshoe arches, zellige tiles, minarets on every roof, domes, palms, adobe.
- Vivid painted streets in scenes from the 1920s–50s (use limewash).
- Shrine interiors and people at prayer.
- Readable Arabic, Ge'ez or Latin signage (models produce gibberish). Titles are added in code.
- Khat-stupor or "exotic" framing, and voyeuristic shots of women.
- Enamel plates on walls before the 1990s; acrylic or tourist baskets in early eras.
- The Harar Gate presented as an original gate.
- Women at sewing machines as the norm on today's Makina Girgir.

## Questions only the family can answer (tracked as `pending-family` in storyboard fact_flags)
- Where the great-grandparents came from, and what dress, faith and possessions follow from that.
- Hand-crank or treadle machine; whether they worked on Makina Girgir.
- What the grandparents traded and where.
- House type: Harari *gegar*, Indian-style, or outside the walls.
- Clothing and headscarves per generation.
- What the mother sold, her school, and whether home had kerosene or electric light.
- Religious practice and which objects are appropriate to show.
- Family photographs, kept as private reference only and never uploaded to free endpoints.
